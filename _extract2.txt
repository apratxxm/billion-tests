Societal Impact — Society, Women & Rural Communities
Impact on Society
BillionTests addresses one of India’s most critical public health failures: the near-complete absence of preventive health screening in underserved communities. By deploying AI-powered diagnostics at the community level, the platform enables early detection of diabetes, kidney disease, UTIs, and liver conditions — conditions that are silent until they become life-threatening. This shifts the healthcare paradigm from reactive, high-cost treatment to proactive, low-cost prevention. At 50,000+ screenings per year, BillionTests prevents an estimated 2,000+ individuals from progressing to advanced-stage disease, reducing hospitalisation burden on the public health system and generating significant societal savings in lost productivity and avoidable mortality.
Impact on Women
Women in rural India bear a disproportionate burden of undiagnosed disease. Approximately 24% of rural women experience UTIs annually, while pregnancy-related kidney and glucose complications remain largely undetected due to absence of affordable, accessible testing. BillionTests specifically prioritises women in its outreach model: health camps are deployed at Anganwadi centres, self-help group (SHG) meetings, and women’s panchayat sessions — spaces where women already gather. Screening is non-invasive, takes under 20 seconds, and is conducted with dignity in community settings. By bringing diagnostics directly to women rather than requiring them to travel 20–50 km to labs, BillionTests removes the two primary barriers: cost and mobility. Women also gain a printed, blockchain-secured health record — many for the first time in their lives — enabling continuity of care during referrals and pregnancies.
A Critical Problem Solved: Pregnancy Monitoring & ASHA Workers
ASHA workers today conduct urine strip tests for pregnant women as part of ANC (Antenatal Care) protocols — but the process is deeply flawed. The conventional method requires the worker to manually match wet strip colours against an 80-shade printed colour chart, often under poor lighting conditions, in homes or outdoors. This process is error-prone (colour perception varies by person, lighting, and strip age), time-consuming (taking 3–5 minutes per reading vs. 20 seconds with BillionTests), and frequently leads to misclassification of glucose or protein levels that are critical indicators during pregnancy. A missed positive on gestational diabetes or preeclampsia (detected via protein in urine) can have life-threatening consequences for the mother and foetus. BillionTests eliminates this entirely: the AI reads the strip with 98%+ accuracy in 20 seconds — removing human subjectivity, colour-chart dependency, and reporting errors from the most critical touchpoint in rural maternal health.
Non-Invasive Testing: A Game-Changer vs. Blood-Based Diagnostics
Conventional blood-based diagnostic testing — the current standard — is invasive, inconvenient, and logistically prohibitive for rural and underserved communities. Blood draws require trained phlebotomists, sterile needles, cold-chain storage (blood samples must be kept at 2–8°C), and timely transport to labs — a complex chain that breaks down constantly in rural India. For many patients, particularly women and the elderly, needles are a source of genuine fear and avoidance, resulting in large-scale non-compliance with recommended tests. Blood tests also require fasting (for glucose), limiting when and where they can be conducted. BillionTests urine-based screening requires none of this: the patient simply dips a strip in a fresh urine sample (first-morning urine is NOT required — any time of day works), places it on the BT Card, and scans with a smartphone. No needles. No cold chain. No fasting. No lab. No wait. Result in 20 seconds, anywhere, anytime. This is what makes community-level mass screening genuinely possible for the first time.
Special Focus: Pregnant Women & Women 50+ — The Highest-Need Cohorts
BillionTests is a transformative tool for two critically underserved female cohorts: (1) Pregnant women, for whom routine urine screening is already mandated under ANC protocols but rarely conducted accurately due to the colour-chart problem described above. Gestational diabetes affects 10–14% of Indian pregnancies; preeclampsia (protein in urine) affects 5–8% and is a leading cause of maternal mortality. BillionTests enables every ANC visit to include an accurate, instant urine screen — at a cost of ₹10 per test. (2) Women aged 50+ face a convergence of three highly prevalent and largely silent conditions: UTI (affecting 30–40% of post-menopausal women annually), Type 2 diabetes (20–25% prevalence), and early chronic kidney disease (15–20% prevalence). These conditions are interconnected — untreated UTI accelerates kidney damage in diabetic women — and BillionTests screens all three simultaneously in a single 20-second test. In a country where women routinely deprioritise their own health, an accessible, non-invasive, instant, and community-delivered screening is not just convenient — it is the only approach that will achieve actual uptake.
Impact on Rural Areas
For rural India, BillionTests operates as a “diagnostic infrastructure layer” that previously did not exist. Tribal districts such as Koraput, Malkangiri, Rayagada, and Sundargarh (Odisha) have virtually no routine screening infrastructure — the platform fills this gap without requiring hospitals, labs, or trained medical professionals. Panchayat-level implementation creates a permanent, rotating community health touchpoint that improves health literacy, encourages preventive behaviour, and generates real-time population health data that district health authorities can act upon. The result is measurable improvement in rural health outcomes at the block and panchayat level — contributing to district-level NHM targets and enabling data-driven public health planning.
Why BillionTests is Near-Perfect for Rural India
Every constraint of rural India has been accounted for in BillionTests’ design. The platform requires no electricity (smartphone battery only), no internet (offline-first; syncs when connectivity is available), no cold chain (urine strips are stable at room temperature), no lab (results delivered on-device), no trained medical staff (icon-guided app in regional languages; half-day training), no expensive hardware (any Android phone; ₹1,999 optional box), and no first-morning urine (any-time sample is sufficient). The entire user journey — from strip dip to result to blockchain-secured digital record — takes under 30 seconds and can be conducted at an Anganwadi centre, a panchayat building, a home visit, or a roadside health camp. This is health infrastructure that goes to the patient, rather than requiring the patient to travel to it. In a country where the average rural resident lives 20–50 km from a diagnostic lab, this is not an incremental improvement — it is a fundamentally different paradigm. The simplicity is deliberate and complete: there is virtually no barrier remaining between a rural individual and an accurate health screening.

BillionTests provides Image Acquisition Systems (devices) to 50 empanelled public and private health centres. Centre staff are trained once (half-day) and thereafter scan independently — extending programme reach continuously between health camp rotations.
